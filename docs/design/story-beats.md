# Story beats: all eight chapters

**Status:** Approved for now (Dan, 2026-10-25); expect adjustments as the game is built. Written 2026-10-18; updated 2026-10-25 with Dan's answers and the M1 decisions made since (floor plan, M1 words, the bedside lamp). Made with the [narrative-craft skill](../../.claude/skills/narrative-craft/SKILL.md), as a story pass across the whole game so that M1 doesn't paint M2 into a corner and every object has one lifeline. A line is `decided` only where it says so, with Dan's date; everything else is `proposed`.

**What this is:** one beat sheet per chapter, in play order. Each one says what the player should feel, what happens, where the moment of levity is, what the House looks like before and after, and what the journal records. It says *where words go and what job they do*; it doesn't write them (CLAUDE.md: Claude drafts story words only when Dan asks).

**What it defers to:** the rooms and House objects are in [house-floor-plan.md](house-floor-plan.md) and the [continuity ledger](continuity-ledger.md); the M1 words are in [m1-words-draft.md](m1-words-draft.md); the Partner is in [partner-song-and-beat-chart.md](partner-song-and-beat-chart.md); the Loss's run and car are in [the-loss-run-and-car.md](the-loss-run-and-car.md) (settled for M1, Dan, 2026-10-25). Where this file and those disagree, those win. This pass's new and changed ledger rows are in [continuity-ledger-additions.md](continuity-ledger-additions.md), to merge into the ledger once Dan has agreed them.

Months follow [ADR-0009](../adr/0009-full-term-pregnancy-timeline.md) (a full-term pregnancy, Dan, 2026-10-25).

**The spine:** Leo is a fixer. Each chapter gives him something to *do* (keep time, clear the way, follow the protocol, beat the paperwork, tune, hold up, sort), and the story keeps showing him that what matters is being there. He ends the game doing the two things that are about presence, not fixing: listening (the Tuner's knobs) and keeping time with someone (the Partner's rhythm).

**Three motifs** (rows in the ledger additions):

- **The heartbeat.** Maya's heartbeat is the Partner's rhythm. At the Loss the light goes out. The Tuner finds the baby's heartbeat through static. The Arrival keeps time with it. Two, then none, then three.
- **The nightstand.** The bedside lamp is turned off to start the Loss and stays off. The home doppler sits on the same nightstand, and the Tuner finds the heartbeat there. The place where the worst night began is where the good news comes back.
- **The fridge.** The hum (the House's mood), Maya's planning note, the family's food after the Loss, the IVF medication, then the scan picture on its door. The most ordinary object holds the most private things.

These sit beside the floor plan's own motifs: the keys and the front door (the Arrival only), and the shut door at the end of the hall.

## 2. The Protector, the Loss (M1)

**Feeling:** before, warmth. Now: being capable and needed, then unable to help. After: the gray House and the long numb stretch.

**Month:** 2.

**Decided (Dan, 2026-10-19):** the Loss starts by turning off the bedside lamp to go to sleep. The loss happens in play, in the run, through no fault of the player's. The quiet car in the rain comes after the run, later the same night, and the player comes back in through the front door to the dropped keys. The keys start only the Arrival.

**Dan's note, 2026-10-25:** the drive to the first scan is still a possible errand, and the drive itself could have been the game, but a car runner risks reading as a car accident, so the run must not be a car. Not locked in. This pulls against the lamp decision, which makes the run the night itself. **Decided (Dan, 2026-10-25):** the night of the Loss. The lamp and the night stay, the car comes after the run, and the scan is not used in the Loss ([the-loss-run-and-car.md](the-loss-run-and-car.md) call 12).

**The run and the car:** settled for M1 in [the-loss-run-and-car.md](the-loss-run-and-car.md) (Dan, 2026-10-25). "Clear the way": a 3D run seen from behind, like Temple Run; the player clears the path for a small light, it works, and the light goes out on a clear stretch after the storm and the song stop. It is not a car.

**House after (decided for M1):** gray, rain, the keys dropped in the entry, the test and onesie gone from view, the lamp off, the spare room door shut.

**Journal (M2):** records the date and the facts. One wording (the Silence has no words). Dated month 2.

**Checks (roadmap M1 item 4, Stories 2.1–2.4):** most testers describe the Loss in emotional words, not "unfair"; nobody looks for a retry out of frustration; nobody calls it cheap; no warning, skip or retry; an authored path, not random; the light fades even after perfect play; reduced motion removes hit-shake; leaving mid-run restarts it and writes no save; the House reads grief for every player. **If item 4 fails, the Loss is fixed before anything else.**

## 3. The Novice, the Long Haul (M2)

**Feeling:** before, grief. Now: numb, then absurd, then frantic, then exhausted. After: cautious hope, then the bills.

**Month:** 3–9.

**House before:** a story beat first, **the decision**. A clinic folder on the kitchen table; tapping it plays a short, quiet beat where they decide to try IVF (FR11's example). After it, the medication box is in the fridge (ledger), the chapter object. Its first micro-game is "OPEN THE BOX!", so the House-to-chapter step is seamless.

**Chapter beats:**
1. **The Injection** (Story 3.4, FR6) opens the chapter. Maya asks Leo to help with her first shot; he is afraid of hurting her. **Stoic/Vulnerable line here.** Set in the chapter's own kitchen at night (a chapter can't reach into the House).
2. **Numb** (early months): slow, gray, quiet micro-games; inputs feel confused.
3. **Frantic** (middle months): colour floods in, faster games, the shouts start.
4. **Exhausted** (last stretch): inputs sometimes don't take.
5. **The result:** a positive test after IVF. No replay of the Partner.

**House after:** colour bleeds back; the rain stops; the couch blanket is gone. The second test on the en-suite counter, where the first one was. The keys move to a basket on the kitchen counter (ledger). The boxes in front of the office are gone: the bills on the desk are the next object.

**Words (Dan):** the decision beat, the Injection with two versions of Leo's line, one- or two-word shouts.

**Journal:** six months in one entry: the retrievals, the shots, the test. Wording follows the Injection answer. Months 3–9.

**Checks (Stories 3.1–3.5):** starts slow, quiet and gray; three micro-games in the first batch ("DON'T FAINT!" is Later); one gesture and a short shout each; three phases of input; reduced motion turns colour changes into cuts or slow fades; 10–15 minutes on a phone; the UI doesn't explain the joke; one ending; the save keeps the loss in the past and the pregnancy now.

## 4. The Provider, the Cost (M2)

**Feeling:** before, cautious hope. Now: trapped, small, angry at paper. After: quiet joy (the Tuner), which only lands because this was so tight.

**Month:** 10.

**Decided (Dan, 2026-10-25): the "one injury" is the Loss.** Among the IVF and new-pregnancy bills is the bill for the night of the Loss, month 2. The memory of the loss stays with you, and the paperwork brings it back. Dan's shape for it: the bills are ridiculous, then one of them stops Leo, a moment of reflection, and then the absurdity of the system comes back around it. It must be handled carefully because of how sensitive it is.

**Chapter beats** (three acts, Story 4.3):
1. **The Invoices:** simple and readable, with absurd line items. Leo is good at this; the fixer's confidence.
2. **The Insurance:** harder documents, read in the large-type reader. The bill for the night of the Loss turns up here, in billing language. **The pause:** the desk goes quiet for a moment; the Due Date doesn't press, nothing drains. Stamping it is the player's choice of when.
3. **The Audit:** the absurdity comes back, harder and funnier (the system asks for proof of things that can't be proved). The Due Date runs; the Bank Account drains.
4. **The result:** the bills are dealt with, or the Due Date runs out. The story moves on either way, with the same journal entry.

**Care points for the bill from the Loss** (proposed): the game never jokes *about* the bill or the loss; the humour is always about the system around it. The bill uses the cold words a real one would, and the UI adds nothing. It can't be stamped wrong in a way that costs money. Whatever the player stamps, the result is the same.

**House after:** tight and anxious; the House creaks (FR31); the fridge hum lower and louder. Bills filed on the desk. The home doppler on the bedside nightstand is the next object.

**Words (Dan):** the bills (document text) and a few off-screen lines from Maya in the "did you pay the gas bill?" style. No Stoic/Vulnerable line recommended.

**Journal:** the money and what it felt like, including that bill. One wording. Month 10.

**Checks (Stories 4.1–4.4):** large type replaces the page; "can't enlarge this page." when it can't; stamps on the desk with an ink thunk; the bank drop shown by number and track, not colour alone; three acts; the bills follow one story; the UI never says the system is confusing; the Due Date can't trap the player; 10–15 minutes on a phone; no replay of conception.

## 5. The Tuner (M2)

**Feeling:** before, trapped. Now: straining to hear someone you haven't met, then hearing them. After: the weight of preparing.

**Month:** 12 (about 12 weeks: the first time a home doppler can find a heartbeat).

**House before:** a home doppler on the nightstand where the lamp was turned off. Couples who have had a loss often buy one, because waiting weeks between scans is unbearable.

**Chapter beats:** static, two knobs, no labels → **a false signal (levity):** a steady rhythm comes through and it's the fridge hum → closer, a faster pulse under the noise → through: the baby's heartbeat, and the picture clears (the scan image).

**House after:** soft morning light; the scan picture on the fridge door; the spare room door ajar again (hearing the baby makes it real); a flat-pack crib box in the entry, the next object.

**Words:** none recommended; the heartbeat is the line.

**Journal:** hearing the heartbeat. One wording. Month 12.

**Checks (Stories 5.1–5.2):** two knobs, each at least 44px; the waveform is the only reading; no wrong popup; static stays when off the signal; it can always be finished; 10–15 minutes on a phone; the House changes the same way for every player.

## 6. The Anchor (M2)

**Feeling:** before, quiet joy. Now: the weight of carrying the new and the fear of dropping it. After: the gentle letting go of the Mourner.

**Month:** 17 (about 8 months pregnant).

**The Panic** (Story 7.1, FR6): tapping the crib box plays the story beat in the living room. Leo builds the crib without instructions; it goes wrong; Maya laughs. **Stoic/Vulnerable line here.** Then the chapter starts.

**Why the crib is built in the living room:** the spare room still holds Leo's old life and, behind it, the memory box. He does the job he *can* do and avoids the room he can't face. A real couple would clear the room first; the stretch is the point, and the Mourner pays it off.

**The load** (Dan's call): *recommended: the crib*, carried through a storm-and-clutter version of the House (the chapter's own world). Alternatives: Maya herself (too literal, and it makes her cargo), or a stack of everything so far (it explains the metaphor).

**Chapter beats:** steady (two hold zones lean Leo left and right) → the storm (falls happen; none ends the chapter) → after the third fall, "You've got this." and the unlabelled assist → the crib set down safely.

**House after:** the crib built in the living room by the window; evening lamplight. Leo's things in the spare room are the next object.

**Words (Dan):** the Panic with two versions of Leo's line. "You've got this." is already in the GDD.

**Journal:** the crib and the weight. Wording follows the Panic answer. Month 17.

**Checks (Stories 7.1–7.3):** two hold zones, each at least 44px, the same on phone and browser; no stick, trigger or stability meter; the load touching the ground keeps the player in the chapter; "You've got this." after the third fall, then help with no badge or label; reduced motion removes hit-shake; 10–15 minutes on a phone; the scripted-wobble-vs-physics test in M2.

**Month:** 17.

**The memory box** (Dan, 2026-10-25: fine for now, come back to it): *proposed:* kept, closed, on the nursery's high shelf; the onesie stays in it, so it stays the first baby's.

**House after:** the spare room is the nursery; clean, warm daylight; the hospital bag by the front door; the keys in their basket. The bag is the next object.

## 8. The Arrival (M2)

**Feeling:** before, quiet readiness. Now: a rush, then two skills used once more, for real. After: home, three of them.

**Month:** 18 (full term).

**House before:** the hospital bag by the front door; the keys in the kitchen basket. Leaving through the front door with the keys, the only time in the game.

**Chapter beats:**
1. **Out the door (levity):** Maya's bag is over-packed (her research, her worry). Brief.
2. **Hear the cry** (Story 8.1): static, the Tuner's knobs, and the cry comes through.
3. **Hold the hand** (Story 8.2). **Decided (Dan, 2026-10-25): the baby's hand.** Tapping in time with the baby's heartbeat or breath. The Partner kept time with Maya's heartbeat; the last rhythm is three of them.
4. **Home:** back in the House, the last room is the nursery.

**Dan's photos (to come):** Dan may share photos from their baby book, some taken in the hospital, as pictures the Arrival can include or evoke, as a love letter to his wife. When they arrive they shape this chapter's look and the last House shot. Until then nothing here is final.

**House after (the last shot), proposed:** the nursery at dawn, the baby monitor's small light on, the baby heard breathing through it. No baby model (FR30: the baby is those skills, not a cutscene-only model). No credits menu, no game-over (Story 8.3).

**Words:** very few; none after the cry recommended.

**Journal:** the birth. One wording. Month 18.

**Checks (Stories 8.1–8.3):** the same waveform and clear-the-static as the Tuner, no wrong popup; Perfect, Good or Miss near the hit, a miss still continues; hits line up with the sound on a phone; the House is the last room; one ending; the save is written between chapters.
