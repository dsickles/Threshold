# Story beats: all eight chapters (draft)

**Status:** Draft for Dan to edit, 2026-10-18. Not decided. Made with the narrative-craft skill, as a story pass across the whole game so that M1 (Partner, Loss) doesn't paint M2 into a corner and every object has one lifeline. Every line here is `proposed` until Dan agrees.

**What this is:** one beat sheet per chapter, in play order, plus the House story beats between them. Each sheet says what the player should feel, what happens, where the one moment of levity is, what the House looks like before and after, and what the journal entry records. It says *where words go and what job they do*; it never writes them. Dialogue, journal wording and on-screen text are Dan's.

**What this is not:** a build plan (that's [roadmap.md](../roadmap.md)) or a floor plan. The rooms and the M1 objects come from the floor-plan draft owned by the "M1 prep: House and Partner song" thread (`m1-prep/house-floor-plan.md` and `m1-prep/continuity-ledger.md` in the project files). This pass doesn't change those files. Its new and changed ledger rows are in [continuity-ledger-additions.md](continuity-ledger-additions.md), for that thread to merge once Dan has answered.

Built from: [gdd.md](gdd.md), [narrative-design.md](narrative-design.md), [requirements.md](requirements.md) FR5–FR33, [stories/](stories/), [scope.md](../scope.md), [ADR-0004](../adr/0004-journal-wording-varies-no-mastery.md), [ADR-0005](../adr/0005-ai-assets-text-dialogue-v1.md), the 2026-09-22 rulings, and the floor-plan, ledger and Partner song drafts.

Dan's calls are collected at the end, each with a recommended answer.

**The spine:** Leo is a fixer. Each chapter gives him something he tries to *do* (keep time, run, follow the protocol, beat the paperwork, tune, hold up, sort), and the story keeps showing him that what matters is being there. He finishes the game by doing the two things he learned that are about presence, not fixing: listening (the Tuner) and keeping time with someone (the Partner).

**Three motifs carry the whole game** (rows in the ledger additions):

- **The heartbeat.** Maya's heartbeat is the Partner's rhythm. A heartbeat is what isn't there at the Loss (the runner's pulse goes quiet before it fades). The Tuner finds the baby's heartbeat through static. The Arrival keeps time with it. First two people, then nothing, then three.
- **The nightstand.** The first test, then the second test in the same spot, then the doppler. One small surface where good news lands.
- **The fridge.** The hum (the House's mood), the IVF medication, then the ultrasound picture on its door. The most ordinary object in the House holds the most private things.

These sit beside the floor plan's own motifs: the keys and the front door, and the shut door at the end of the hall.

### A timeline problem to settle first

FR17 puts the IVF positive test at the end of month 9 and the Arrival at month 14. That is a pregnancy of about five months. A real one is about nine. The journal pages are dated, so a player who has been through this will notice, and the audience includes exactly those people.

**Recommended fix** (Dan's call 1): keep months 0–9 as they are, and stretch the pregnancy to its real length:

| Chapter | FR17 now | Recommended | Why that month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Provider | 10 | 10 | The bills land early: the IVF cycles and the hospital visit from the Loss |
| Tuner | 11–13 | 12 | About 12 weeks pregnant: the first time a home doppler can find a heartbeat |
| Anchor | 11–13 | 17 | About 8 months pregnant: Maya can't lift; the crib goes up |
| Mourner | 11–13 | 17 | Nesting, the last weeks |
| Arrival | 14 | 18 | Term |

This keeps the play order and FR33's pacing (the quiet Mourner after the stressful Provider). It is a change to a decided line (FR17 and decision-history §4, "about a 14-month timeline"), so it needs an ADR or a dated scope line if Dan agrees. The beat sheets below use the recommended months in brackets where they differ.

## 0. Opening beat: moving day (M1)

**Feeling:** before, nothing (the title). Now: standing in a cold house that isn't theirs yet, with someone you love. After: the Partner's warmth.

**Where:** porch → entry → living room (l1), per the floor plan. The beat plays at l1.

**Beats:**
1. The first tap crosses the threshold. The House is cold: bare bulbs, boxes, the fridge hum.
2. At the living room, Maya is heard as subtitles from the next room. The words establish three things: their couple shorthand, Leo as the fixer (he wants to sort the boxes, the light, the plan), and that Leo's old things went "in the back room for now". That line plants the spare room as *the room for later* without saying so.
3. **The Stoic/Vulnerable line.** Maya says something about what this house is for. Leo either deflects (a joke, a practical answer) or answers openly. Either way the next step is the same.
4. The boxes in the back-hall doorway are cleared; a lamp shows in the bedroom; the record player crackles.

**Levity:** the shorthand itself, and Maya's small anxious detail (FR: "did we lock the back door?"-type worry) on day one.

**Words (Dan):** about 6–10 short lines, plus the two versions of Leo's line. Where: subtitles at l1.

**Journal:** none yet. The journal exists from the start as a blank dated page (Story 1.7); it's in M2.

## 1. The Partner (M1)

**Feeling:** before, cold uncertainty. Now: giddy, clumsy closeness. After: the shock of the Loss, which only works because this was so warm.

**Month:** 0.

**House before:** the record player on its box in the main bedroom, the one warm light.

**Chapter beats** (the screen, song and chart are in the Partner song draft; this is the story they tell):
1. **Close** (intro and A): a single heartbeat; tapping along is listening to Maya. Shy, slow.
2. **Playful** (B): the band comes in. Missed taps are laughter, never mocking.
3. **The stumble** (bar 48): the music stops for a bar. A human pause, not a failure.
4. **Build and crescendo** (C, D): joy, the screen fills with light.
5. **The result:** a positive pregnancy test.

**How the test arrives:** the chapter ends on the light; the test is *found* back in the House, on the main bedroom nightstand. No card, no text. The player discovers the news the way people do.

**Levity:** the laughter on misses and the stumble.

**House after:** warm (`house-hope`). Test on the nightstand. Record player lid closed. Spare room door ajar: a cleared corner, the onesie on the windowsill. The car keys on the entry table become the next object.

**Words (Dan):** none in the chapter (instrumental, wordless laughs). Optional: one or two subtitle lines from Maya when the test is found. Recommended: none; the object is enough.

**Journal (M2):** records the night they moved in and the test. Wording follows the opening beat's Stoic/Vulnerable answer.

**Checks (from roadmap M1 and Story 1.2):** the first object found by 4 of 5 cold testers within about 3 minutes; nobody says the rhythm counted a tap wrong; hits line up with the sound on a phone; a miss never stops the song; one ending; the House warms the same way for every player; no keepsake for a better performance.

## 2. The Protector, the Loss (M1)

**Feeling:** before, warmth. Now: joy, then confusion, panic and silence, and nothing the player does matters. After: the gray House, and the long numb stretch.

**Month:** 2 (about 8 weeks).

**House before:** warm. The car keys on the entry table are tappable. Nothing warns: the keys are the most ordinary object in the House (no warning, no skip, no title card, Story 2.1).

**The errand** (Dan's call 4): **recommended: the drive to the first scan.** It is the most ordinary errand an 8-week couple has, so tapping the keys feels like good news coming. It gives the euphoric first stretch something true to talk about, and it puts the Silence somewhere real: the car park afterwards.

**Chapter beats:**
1. **Euphoria:** a sunny, clear path. The couple plans and dreams in subtitles (names, the spare room, who they'll tell). The UI doesn't label the beat.
2. **Confusion:** the path starts to break. Something is off and nobody says what.
3. **Panic:** authored obstacles that can't be avoided. A stumble never ends the run.
4. **The fade:** the runner fades, even on a perfect run. Its pulse (the heartbeat motif) goes quiet a moment before.
5. **The Silence:** the car, parked, rain on the glass. No words, no subtitles, no choice, no skip. The player can look. It lasts as long as it lasts, then the House.

**Levity:** the planning in the first stretch: Maya's over-researched worries, Leo's bad name ideas. It has to be genuinely funny, or the fall doesn't land.

**House after:** gray (`house-grief`), rain on the windows. The test and the onesie are gone from view (into the memory box). The spare room door is shut and can't be tapped. In M1, no next object.

**Words (Dan):** subtitles in the euphoric stretch only, roughly 10–15 short lines. Nothing after the path breaks. No Stoic/Vulnerable line in this chapter (the Silence has no words, Story 2.3).

**Journal (M2):** records the date and the facts. One wording for every player (no line to follow). Dated month 2.

**Conflict to fix:** Story 2.4 says "the crib is put away" after the Loss. The floor-plan draft and ledger say there is no crib until the Anchor. Recommended: the ledger wins (one crib, one lifeline), and Story 2.4's line is updated once Dan agrees the floor plan.

**Checks (roadmap M1 item 4, Stories 2.1–2.4):** most testers describe the Loss in emotional words, not "unfair"; nobody looks for a retry out of frustration; nobody calls it cheap; no warning, skip or retry; the path is authored, not random; the runner fades on a perfect run; reduced motion removes hit-shake; leaving mid-run restarts it and writes no save; the House reads grief for every player. **If item 4 fails, the Loss is fixed before anything else.**

## 3. The Novice, the Long Haul (M2)

**Feeling:** before, grief. Now: numb, then absurd, then frantic, then exhausted. After: cautious hope, and then the bills.

**Month:** 3–9.

**House before** (after the Loss, M2 adds this): a story beat, **the decision**. A clinic folder appears on the kitchen table. Tapping it plays a short, quiet beat where the couple decides to try IVF (FR11's example). Then a delivery box of medication sits by the front door: the chapter object.

**Why the box:** the Novice's first micro-game is "OPEN THE BOX!". The chapter object becomes the first game, so the House-to-chapter step is seamless.

**Chapter beats:**
1. **The Injection** (Story 3.4, FR6): opens the chapter, before the montage. Maya asks Leo to help with her first shot; he is afraid of hurting her. **Stoic/Vulnerable line here.** Recommended setting: the kitchen, at night, inside the chapter's own scene (a chapter can't reach into the House, so it draws its own kitchen).
2. **Numb** (early months): slow, gray, quiet micro-games. Inputs feel confused. No shouting yet.
3. **Frantic** (middle months): colour floods in, the games speed up, the shouts start. This is where it's funny.
4. **Exhausted** (last stretch): inputs sometimes don't take. Six months of retrievals in a few minutes.
5. **The result:** a positive test after IVF. The chapter doesn't replay the Partner.

**Levity:** the micro-games ("INJECT THE BUTT!" is the joke; the UI never explains it).

**House after:** colour bleeds back; the rain stops. The second test sits on the main bedroom nightstand, where the first one was. A sharps bin on the kitchen counter (optional, see ledger additions). The office door opens: bills piled on the desk, the next object.

**Words (Dan):** the decision beat (short), the Injection scene (the longest dialogue scene so far), two versions of Leo's key line. Shouts for the micro-games (one or two words each).

**Journal:** records six months in one entry: the retrievals, the shots, the test. Wording follows the Injection answer. Sits in months 3–9.

**Checks (Stories 3.1–3.5):** starts slow, quiet and gray, no warning; three micro-games in the first batch ("DON'T FAINT!" is Later); one gesture and a short shout each; three phases of input feel; reduced motion turns colour changes into cuts or slow fades; 10–15 minutes on a phone; the UI doesn't explain the joke; one ending; the save keeps the loss in the past and the pregnancy now.

## 4. The Provider, the Cost (M2)

**Feeling:** before, cautious hope. Now: trapped, small, angry at paper. After: quiet joy (the Tuner), which only lands because this was so tight.

**Month:** 10.

**House before:** the office, newly opened, bills piled on the desk. Real-life logic: the spare bedroom they weren't using becomes the place Leo deals with money. The fixer gets a room for providing at the moment the story turns to cost.

**The "one injury"** (FR24, Dan's call 5): FR24 says a run of bills should tell one micro-story, "such as a specific injury". **Recommended: the bills for the Loss.** Among the IVF and first-scan bills is the hospital visit from month 2: the scan, the follow-up. Leo has to read it, check it and fight it, eight months later. This is something many couples really go through, it ties the Cost back to the Loss without replaying it, and it makes the paperwork personal without the UI saying so. The alternative, a lighter one, is bills for the IVF and the new pregnancy only.

**Chapter beats** (three acts, Story 4.3):
1. **The Invoices:** simple, readable. Leo is good at this. The fixer's confidence.
2. **The Insurance:** harder documents; the large-type reader is how they're read. The hospital visit from the Loss turns up here, in billing language.
3. **The Audit:** the sequence tightens; the Due Date runs; the Bank Account drains.
4. **The result:** the bills are dealt with, or the Due Date runs out. The story moves on either way, with the same journal entry.

**Levity:** one absurd line item in the Invoices (an authored charge for something tiny), and subtext lines from Maya off-screen in the "did you pay the gas bill?" style the narrative doc describes.

**House after:** tight and anxious: the House creaks (FR31: "it creaks when he is anxious"); the fridge hum is lower and louder. Bills filed in a box on the desk. The home doppler on the main bedroom nightstand is the next object.

**Words (Dan):** the bills themselves (authored documents; these are a lot of words but mostly form text), a few subtext lines from Maya. No Stoic/Vulnerable line recommended (word budget).

**Journal:** records the money and what it felt like. One wording. Dated month 10.

**Checks (Stories 4.1–4.4):** large type replaces the page; "can't enlarge this page." when it can't; stamps on the desk with an ink thunk; the bank drop shown by number and track, not colour alone; three acts; the bills follow one story; the UI never says the system is confusing; the Due Date can't trap the player; 10–15 minutes on a phone; no replay of conception.

## 5. The Tuner (M2)

**Feeling:** before, trapped. Now: straining to hear someone you haven't met, then hearing them. After: the weight of preparing (the Anchor).

**Month:** 11–13 [recommended 12].

**House before:** a home doppler on the nightstand. Real-life logic: couples who have had a loss often buy one, because waiting weeks between scans is unbearable. It's on the nightstand where the tests were.

**Chapter beats:**
1. **Static:** two knobs, a waveform, noise. No labels, no "wrong" popup.
2. **The false signal (levity):** a steady rhythm comes through and it's the fridge hum, or Maya's stomach. The House is in the static.
3. **Closer:** a faster, quieter pulse under the noise.
4. **Through:** the static clears; the baby's heartbeat, and the picture comes through (the ultrasound image). This is the heartbeat motif's turn: the pulse that went quiet at the Loss is back, and it's a new one.

**House after:** soft morning light. The ultrasound picture on the fridge door. The spare room door is ajar again (hearing the baby makes it real; Leo opens the door). Leo's boxes still fill it. A flat-pack crib box has arrived in the entry: the next object.

**Words (Dan):** few or none. Recommended: none in the chapter; the heartbeat is the line.

**Journal:** records hearing the heartbeat. One wording. Sits in months 11–13 [12].

**Checks (Stories 5.1–5.2):** two knobs, each at least 44px; the waveform is the only reading; no wrong popup; static stays when off the signal; the chapter can always be finished; 10–15 minutes on a phone; the House changes the same way for every player.

## 6. The Anchor (M2)

**Feeling:** before, quiet joy. Now: the weight of carrying the new, and the fear of dropping it. After: the gentle letting go of the Mourner.

**Month:** 11–13 [recommended 17].

**House before:** the flat-pack crib box in the entry.

**The Panic** (Story 7.1, FR6): tapping the crib box plays the story beat in the House, in the living room. Leo builds the crib without instructions; it goes wrong; Maya laughs, and the tension breaks. **Stoic/Vulnerable line here.** Then the chapter starts.

**Why the crib is built in the living room, not the nursery:** the spare room is still full of Leo's old life, and the memory box is in there. Leo does the job he *can* do (build a thing) and avoids the room he can't face. A real couple would clear the room first; the stretch is the point, and the Mourner pays it off.

**The load** (Dan's call 6): **recommended: the crib**, carried through a storm-and-clutter version of the House (the chapter's own world, "familiar yet novel"). It's the object the player just watched being built, so its weight is real; it is fragile; and it is literally the new thing he has to carry. Alternatives: Maya herself (too literal, and it makes her cargo), or an abstract stack of everything so far (the bills, the doppler, the box; clever, but it explains the metaphor).

**Chapter beats:**
1. **Steady:** the carry starts calm; two hold zones lean Leo left and right.
2. **The storm:** wind, clutter, the load sways. Falls happen; the chapter never ends on one.
3. **Maya's voice:** after the third fall, "You've got this." (the one line already written in the GDD), then the invisible assist.
4. **Set down:** the crib is set down safely. The storm stops.

**Levity:** the Panic (Maya's laugh), and the crib wobbling in the first stretch.

**House after:** the crib stands built in the living room by the window. Evening lamplight. The next object is Leo's boxes in the spare room.

**Words (Dan):** the Panic scene (the second-longest dialogue scene) and two versions of Leo's line. Maya's line after the third fall is already written ("You've got this.").

**Journal:** records the crib and the weight. Wording follows the Panic answer. Sits in months 11–13 [17].

**Checks (Stories 7.1–7.3):** two hold zones, each at least 44px, the same on phone and browser; no stick, trigger or stability meter; the load touching the ground keeps the player in the chapter; "You've got this." after the third fall, then help with no badge or label; reduced motion removes hit-shake; 10–15 minutes on a phone; the scripted-wobble-vs-physics test happens in M2.

## 7. The Mourner (M2)

**Feeling:** before, effort and weight. Now: slow, quiet, letting go and keeping. After: the Arrival's rush.

**Month:** 11–13 [recommended 17].

**House before:** Leo's old-life boxes in the spare room, the door ajar.

**Chapter beats** (zen unpacking, Story 6.2):
1. **The old life (levity):** Leo's single-guy stuff: games, the old armchair, things he's embarrassed by. Each is placed: kept, stored or given away. Soft placements, household sounds.
2. **The memory box:** under the boxes, the shoebox from after the Loss: the first test and the onesie. The music thins. The player holds it. What happens to it is Dan's call 7; recommended below.
3. **The room:** what's left is an empty room with good light.
4. **The crib comes in:** the last placement is the crib, moved from the living room.

**What remains, the same for every player** (Story 6.3): the nursery with the crib, a baby monitor on the shelf (new, bought for the nursery), the memory box on the high shelf. One piece of Leo's old life stays in the House (recommended: the guitar, moved to the living room corner): growing up isn't erasing yourself. Placement during the chapter snaps to fixed spots, so the layout is identical for everyone (Story 6.3 and FR29's "final placement can persist" both hold).

**Levity:** the embarrassing finds in Leo's boxes. Optional and cheap: the crib's instructions, unopened, turn up at the bottom of the crib box.

**House after:** the spare room is the nursery. Clean, warm daylight. A hospital bag by the front door, with the keys: the next object.

**Words (Dan):** recommended none or almost none in the chapter (acoustic or piano score, Story 6.2); the objects carry it.

**Journal:** records clearing the room and finding the box. One wording. Sits in months 11–13 [17].

**Checks (Stories 6.1–6.3):** pick up and drag with a ghost; an invalid drop snaps back, shown by more than colour; no inventory grid; the UI doesn't label letting go; acoustic or piano score; the same layout for every player whatever order things were placed in; 10–15 minutes on a phone.

## 8. The Arrival (M2)

**Feeling:** before, quiet readiness. Now: a rush, then two skills used once more, for real. After: home, three of them.

**Month:** 14 [recommended 18].

**House before:** the hospital bag and the keys by the front door. The same act that started the Loss: leaving with the keys. Nothing warns in either direction.

**Chapter beats:**
1. **Out the door (levity):** Maya's bag is over-packed (her research, her worry); something from it is absurd. Brief.
2. **Hear the cry** (Story 8.1): static, the knobs from the Tuner, and the cry comes through.
3. **Hold the hand** (Story 8.2, Dan's call 8): **recommended: the baby's hand around Leo's finger**, tapping in time with the baby's heartbeat or breath. The Partner kept time with Maya's heartbeat; the last rhythm is the three of them. Alternative: Maya's hand during labour (strong, but then the baby is only the cry).
4. **Home:** back in the House, the last room is the nursery.

**House after (the last shot):** the nursery at dawn. The baby monitor's small light is on and the baby is heard breathing through it. Recommended: no baby model (FR30: "the baby is those skills, not a cutscene-only model"), so the baby is present as sound, like in the Tuner. The House is the last room; no credits menu, no game-over (Story 8.3). The record player's part in the ending is the floor-plan thread's call 8; this pass would suggest it plays the Partner's melody, softly, in the living room.

**Words (Dan):** very few. Recommended: none after the cry.

**Journal:** the last entry: the birth. One wording. Dated month 14 [18].

**Checks (Stories 8.1–8.3):** the same waveform and clear-the-static as the Tuner, no wrong popup; Perfect, Good or Miss near the hit, a miss still continues; hits line up with the sound on a phone; the House is the last room; one ending; the save is written between chapters.
